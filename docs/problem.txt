2026 MCM Senior Group Problem A
SafeInvest: Building a Trustworthy Robo-Advisor
Background and Challenge
SafeInvest is a fictional fintech company that offers investment advice through a mobile app. New
customers complete a short digital questionnaire, and an automated system recommends one of
three portfolios:
• Conservative,
• Balanced, or
• Growth.
Each portfolio uses equities, government bonds, and a cash-like short-term investment. After a
customer invests, the app reviews the portfolio once per period and may retain the current holdings,
rebalance, change the target allocation, or request review by a human adviser.
Automation can make advice inexpensive and widely available, but it also creates new risks. A
portfolio may be unsuitable for a customer, an algorithm may trade too often, and a recommendation
may be difficult to explain. The system must also recognize conditions in which its model should
not be trusted.
In this problem, we want to develop and test an automated, explainable, and risk-aware robo-
advisory process for SafeInvest. The process must recommend suitable portfolios, adapt them only
when the expected benefit justifies the cost, and identify decisions that require human review.
Customer Information
SafeInvest’s questionnaire records investment horizon, the largest temporary loss in purchasing power
that the customer believes they could tolerate, and liquidity need. Five representative responses are
shown below. Loss tolerance is a customer response, not a guaranteed portfolio limit.

Provided Market Data
The file SafeInvest Synthetic Data.csv contains 180 independent synthetic market scenarios,
each lasting 30 annual periods.
• scenario id: Scenario number, 1–180;
• period : Annual period within the scenario, 1–30;
• equity return : Nominal total equity return;
• bond return : Nominal total government-bond return;
• bill proxy return : Nominal short-term bill proxy return;
• inf lation: Inflation during the period.

Common Conditions
Every market scenario begins with 100 units in bills and has no later deposits or withdrawals.
Portfolios must be long-only and fully invested. SafeInvest may review a portfolio once per period.
Charge 0.25% of the money moved between assets. Count each amount transferred between
assets only once when calculating transaction costs. Moving 20 units from bills to equities therefore
costs 0.05 units. Deduct the transaction cost from portfolio wealth at the time of trading, before
applying that period’s returns. Charge the initial allocation from bills in the same way. Ignore
taxes, other fees, and terminal liquidation costs.
A decision for period t may use only observations through period t − 1 of that scenario. It may
not use period t or any later observation from that scenario.
Questions
Address the following tasks as one coherent investigation.
1. Design the digital suitability process. Choose measures of investment growth and risk.
Express the large-loss measure so that larger values mean greater loss. Your risk assessment must
separate ordinary fluctuations from unusually large losses and must account for purchasing power.
Recommend allocations or allocation rules for the Conservative, Balanced, and Growth portfolios.
Then design a transparent onboarding rule that uses the questionnaire information to recommend
one of the three portfolios or request human review. Apply the rule to Profiles A–E with a brief
explanation.
2. Design and evaluate the automated portfolio policy . For a customer assigned to the
Balanced portfolio, develop a rule that decides whether to keep the current holdings, rebalance,
change the target allocation, or request human review. Trade or change the target only when the
estimated benefit justifies the transaction cost.
Use an equal-weight baseline whose target remains one-third in each asset. Restore these weights
at every review and charge the stated cost. Compare your policy with this baseline across the
supplied scenarios. For each strategy, report the median growth and large-loss results, the 10th
percentile of growth, and the 90th percentile of the large-loss measure. Briefly examine one severe-
loss scenario and report the percentage of review periods in which your policy trades. Decide whether
automated weight updates add value.
Include one customer-facing explanation of no more than 50 words for a trade, target change, or
request for human review.
3. T est reliability and define safeguards. Test one important modeling assumption or the
transaction-cost rate and state whether your main recommendation changes. Identify one important
limitation and one condition that should trigger human review or suspension of the automated policy.
State what the app should do while review is pending.
Conclusion. Give a concise recommendation that defines the three portfolios, assigns Profiles A–
E, states whether the Balanced portfolio should use automated weight updates, and identifies the
main safeguard. When evaluating recommendations for Profiles A–E, take their stated investment
horizons into account. You may use Scenarios 1–120 to develop and adjust your model. Evaluate
your final policy using Scenarios 121–180 without further adjustment.
